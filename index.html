What a mortgage appointment in Slough actually covers

Every appointment is structured around your situation rather than a fixed script, but most first meetings move through the same broad stages. Knowing what is coming makes the hour far less intimidating and means you walk out with next steps, not just information.

  1. 01
    Understanding your position

    Income, existing debt, deposit size, and timeframe are covered first, so any recommendation is grounded in what you can realistically afford rather than what a headline rate implies.

  2. 02
    Mapping the options

    Fixed versus tracker, two-year versus five-year, and the trade-offs between a lower rate and lower fees are explained in plain terms, matched against your plans for the next few years.

  3. 03
    Affordability and lender fit

    Not every lender scores income, credit history, or property type the same way. This stage narrows the field to lenders realistically likely to approve your application.

  4. 04
    A written recommendation

    You leave with a clear summary of the recommended route, the figures behind it, and what documents are needed to move to a formal application.

Remortgaging and moving home

For homeowners coming to the end of a fixed rate, the appointment looks quite different. The focus shifts to comparing the cost of staying with the current lender's follow-on rate against switching, factoring in early repayment charges, product fees, and how much equity has built up since the last valuation. Anyone planning to move home rather than simply remortgage will also look at porting an existing deal versus starting fresh, and how a change in household income affects what is now affordable.

Landlords and buy-to-let

Buy-to-let applications are assessed differently from residential mortgages, with lenders weighing rental income, tax treatment, and portfolio size rather than only personal salary. Appointments for landlords in Slough typically cover whether to hold a property personally or through a limited company, how rental yield in specific postcodes compares to borrowing costs, and how additional purchases affect overall lending capacity across a growing portfolio.

How to prepare before you book

A little preparation turns a good appointment into a genuinely useful one. Recent payslips, bank statements, and a rough idea of monthly outgoings let an adviser give figures that hold up rather than rough estimates that shift later. It also helps to think honestly about priorities before the meeting: is the goal the lowest possible monthly payment, the shortest mortgage term, or the flexibility to overpay if income rises.
